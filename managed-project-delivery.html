Simple definition

The work is managed
as part of the service.

You are not merely introduced to a person and left to turn that introduction into a finished project.

The purchase

Outcome, not access

In a hiring model, the core transaction gives you access to professional time. The result still depends on how well you define the role, choose the person, communicate the brief, make decisions, and manage the schedule.

In managed delivery, the offer describes the deliverables, timing, inputs, included revisions, assumptions, and acceptance criteria. Those terms—not a profile or hourly rate—are the center of the purchase.

The operator

One accountable delivery path

The managed provider determines which capabilities are required, sources the supply, reconciles different professional terms, gives the contractor usable context, tracks the project, answers routine questions, consolidates review, and checks the delivery.

The buyer still makes decisions that materially affect scope, budget, deadline, or creative direction. Routine coordination is not pushed back onto them.

The boundary

Defined completion, not unlimited satisfaction

Creative work contains judgment. A credible managed service cannot promise that every subjective preference will be satisfied forever. It can define objective acceptance criteria, agree a direction, include review rounds, and ensure that files meet the stated production and format requirements.

This makes “done” inspectable while preserving a fair boundary for both buyer and professional.

Short answer

Managed project delivery means the customer approves a complete project offer and receives checked work, while the provider takes responsibility for sourcing and operating the delivery process in between.

Managed project delivery

The service includes the work around the work.

You explain the intended result and provide the context you have. The provider structures it, identifies missing decisions, sources the right capability, presents one coherent offer, manages delivery, checks the files, coordinates included revisions, and escalates genuine exceptions.

  • 02Routine questions stay with the operator
  • 03The result arrives ready for acceptance

Managed delivery questions

What it is.
What it is not.

01Is managed project delivery the same as a freelance marketplace?

No. A marketplace generally helps buyers discover or transact with professionals. A managed delivery service takes responsibility for converting the request into a bounded project and operating the work through acceptance.

02Do I lose control over the project?

No. You approve the complete offer and any material change. The point is to remove routine management, not to hide the scope, price, deadline, or acceptance decision.

03Is this only for large companies?

No. Borage is designed for people and small teams buying bounded creative and specialist work—the same kinds of buyers who might otherwise assemble a freelance project themselves.

04When should I hire directly instead?

Direct hiring can make sense when you already know the right person, want to manage the work closely, need ongoing embedded capacity, or view contractor management as a capability your team wants to own.
